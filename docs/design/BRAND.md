# Cyber City Tactics Brand Book

Oct 26, 2026 · @Jeremy

Cyber City Tactics is a neon brawler RPG about street runners who choose who they hurt. This book sets how the game looks, sounds and talks, everywhere it shows up.

## The brand in one page

**Promise:** a full shadowrun in a pocket. Punch, shoot, cast and hack your way through a rain-soaked megacity, then decide who pays for it.

**Who it's for:** players who grew up on River City Ransom and Shadowrun, and anyone who wants a couch co-op brawler that also has a real RPG under it. They play on a phone on the bus, or on a TV with two controllers.

**Personality:**

| Trait | What it means | What it isn't |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Street-smart | Plain words, specific numbers, a fixer's dry humor | Jargon walls, lore dumps before the first punch |
| Loud and bright | Neon on ink, big type, crunchy hits | Grimdark mud, beige UI, whispering |
| Fair | Every choice shows its cost before you make it | Hidden penalties, gotchas, preachy morality |

**The idea under everything:** *choose who you hurt.* Gangs and corps are fair game; families and corner shops are not, and the city remembers. Every system, from heat stars to door-sign colors, makes that choice visible.

## The world

Seattle, 2079. Magic came back, dragons came with it, and corporations grew big enough to buy the sky. On paper it's still a city; on the street it's **the Sprawl**.

- **Where:** six districts, from the Rustline Barrens under a dead monorail through a ghoul night market to the Kagemori Arcology and the Matrix host inside it. Between jobs, runners walk **the Den**, a street hub of fixers, docs and fences.
- **Who runs it:** Kagemori, a megacorp that builds cyberware and security drones, and something older behind it.
- **The mystery:** data chips stamped with a paper lantern. Twenty-four lore shards, found across every mode, reveal Project LANTERN and the dragon Ssareth, "who lights the way." Players earn the full story; we never front-load it.
- **Magic and chrome sit side by side.** A samurai with a cyberarm and a shaman with a bound fire spirit fight on the same street. Neither is the "cool" one.

**Rules for new content:** keep it street-level (people, blocks, jobs, not galactic stakes); give every place one concrete detail (a noodle stand, a brand of soycaf, a gang's tag); and let the lantern stay a whisper until the player goes looking.

## Name and wordmark

**The name** is always *Cyber City Tactics*, three words, title case in running text. The short name is *Cyber City* (home-screen label, tight spaces). Never "CCT" in player-facing copy, and never the old name, Chrome & Sorcery.

**The wordmark** is type, not a drawing: CYBER CITY set in Teko SemiBold, all caps, with TACTICS knocked out of a Neon Pink bar beneath it. The bar is the brand's one signature shape; it reads as a neon sign, a health bar and a highlighter at once. CYBER CITY carries a 3 px chromatic split: Neon Pink offset left, Mana Cyan offset right, like a sign seen through rain. This is the lockup on the game's title screen.

| Lockup | Use it for | Built from |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Stacked (primary) | Title screen, store art, posters | CYBER CITY over a Neon Pink bar holding TACTICS, left-aligned |
| Horizontal | Headers, social banners, the README | CYBER CITY then the TACTICS bar on one line |
| Title text | Places the bar can't go (plain text, small UI) | "Cyber City Tactics" in Teko, Text color |

- **Clear space:** the height of the TACTICS bar on every side.
- **Minimum size:** CYBER CITY at least 24 px tall on screen (the bar text stays readable down to 11 px).
- **Backgrounds:** Ink or Panel only. On a photo or busy art, put the lockup on an Ink plate at 85% opacity.
- **Don't:** recolor the bar (pink only), outline or bevel the letters, add a glow wider than the bar's own height, set it in another face, stretch it, or rotate it.

## Color

Neon on ink: a near-black violet ground, light that comes from signs, and five bright hues that each mean one thing. A player should be able to read the game state from color alone.

| Name | Hex | Job | Seen in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ink | #0C0A14 | The night. Every screen's ground | Backgrounds, text on Neon Pink |
| Panel | #17121F | Raised surfaces | Cards, menus |
| Panel 2 | #1F1830 | Selected and hovered surfaces | Selected tabs, update bar |
| Line | #30283F | Borders and dividers | Card edges, ghost buttons |
| Text | #EFE9FF | Primary text, slightly violet | Headings, body |
| Muted | #A79DC2 | Secondary text | Hints, labels, descriptions |
| Neon Pink | #FF3D8B | The brand and the main action; also danger to you | Primary buttons, the wordmark bar, your HP, bad buildings |
| Mana Cyan | #45D2FF | Magic, tech, hacking | Mana bar, spells, ICE, hacking UI |
| Street Gold | #FFC63D | Money, rewards, attention | Creds, feats, specials, rare items |
| Signal Green | #7DFFB0 | Safe, good, done | Fair-game doors, healing, the safehouse |
| Karma Violet | #B98CFF | Progress and the hidden | Karma, lore, secrets, the Lantern |

**Supporting colors**, used only for their one meaning: Fire Orange #FF6A3D (fire, explosions, burn), Siren Red #FF3D4A (heat stars, cops), Legendary Orange #FF8A3D (Black-market items).

**Ratio:** about 80% Ink and Panel, 15% Text and Muted, 5% neon. One neon leads per screen; the others are accents. Never set neon text on neon.

**Contrast:** Text and Muted on Ink pass WCAG AA (Muted on Ink is about 7:1). Text on Neon Pink buttons is Ink, not white. Never carry meaning by color alone: door signs also say the building's name, heat uses star shapes, and rarity is in the item's name.

## Typography

Two Google Fonts, both free for commercial use. **Teko** is the street sign: tall, condensed, loud, always for short words. **Chakra Petch** is the readout: squared-off and technical, comfortable at small sizes.

| Role | Face | Size / line height | Case and tracking | Used for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Display | Teko SemiBold 600 | 48–84 px / 0.82 | Title case, +0.01em | Screen titles, the wordmark |
| Heading | Teko SemiBold 600 | 32–48 px / 0.9 | Title case, +0.02em | Menu headings |
| Subhead | Teko SemiBold 600 | 22 px / 1.0 | Caps, +0.04em, Muted | Section labels |
| In-game HUD | Teko SemiBold 600 | 11–15 px (canvas) | Caps, dark outline | Names, levels, creds, banners |
| Body | Chakra Petch 400 | 15 px / 1.5 | Sentence case | Menu text, dialogue |
| Button and tab | Chakra Petch SemiBold 600 | 11.5–13 px | Caps, +0.06–0.08em | Buttons, tabs |
| Eyebrow | Chakra Petch 400 | 11 px | Caps, +0.18em, Muted | The line above a title ("Safehouse · block 3") |

- Display sizes use `clamp()` so titles scale with the screen; never set a title smaller than 32 px.
- Teko for words, never for paragraphs. Chakra Petch for anything longer than one line.
- Numbers that change (creds, ammo, scores) use tabular figures so they don't jitter.
- Fallbacks: Teko → Impact → Arial Narrow; Chakra Petch → the system UI font. The game must stay readable offline before the fonts load.

## Voice and tone

The game talks like a good fixer: short, specific, a little dry, and always straight about the cost. Characters have flavor; the UI does not.

**Two registers:**

- **Characters** (Juno, Kit, Pixel, Old Wen) speak in first person, with attitude, in two or three sentences. They can joke; they never lecture.
- **System text** (buttons, tips, cards) is plain, active and exact: what happens, what it costs, how to undo it.

| Do | Don't |
| --- | --- |
| "Breaking in here hurts people who did nothing to you. Heat +3, cred −10." | "Are you sure? This action may have consequences." |
| "Tap HACK while the cursor is inside the green window." | "Utilize the hacking interface to breach the lock." |
| "A new version is ready. Update now" | "An update has been detected and is pending installation." |
| "My people dragged you back to the safehouse. The medics took ¥120." | "You died. 30% of credits were deducted." |
| "Bag full" | "Inventory capacity exceeded" |

**Rules:**

- Buttons say exactly what happens: *Hack the door*, *Lay low · ¥180*, *Get back up*. Prices go on the button.
- Numbers beat adjectives: "+20% gun damage", not "much stronger guns".
- Name things the way players do: *creds (money)*, *heat*, street *cred*, *karma*, *the Den*. Never internal names.
- Morality is shown, not preached: state the cost and let the player choose.
- No exclamation marks in system text. Characters may use one, rarely.
- Write for touch and keys both: "Tap ENTER" on phones, "Press J" on keyboards, button names on controllers.

**Archetypes** (Runner, Samurai, Mage, Gunslinger, Decker, Shaman, Rigger) are told apart by gear and specials, not body type. Any runner can be anyone.

## Visual language

Everything is drawn in code as flat vector shapes on a 480 × 270 stage. There are no sprites and no photos, which keeps the look consistent and the download tiny.

- **Figures:** chunky limbs, big heads, a visor or prop that tells you who they are. Two-tone coats (base and shadow), one accent color. Elites add a colored aura and a name tag.
- **The street:** three parallax layers of buildings with lit windows, a sidewalk band, and a floor with perspective lines. The playable floor is a band of lanes; depth is real, so dodging a beam means changing lanes.
- **Signs are the light source.** Neon signs (NOODLE, PAWN, 24H, CYBERDOC) are the brightest thing on screen and the main way a place says what it is. A building's door frame color tells its tier; its sign color tells if it's fair game.
- **Weather:** rain in the streets, none indoors. Districts shift the sky color and the sign set, never the drawing style.
- **The HUD** lives in the corners in Teko caps with a dark outline: you top left, the place and the job top right, a minimap along the bottom in the Sprawl. In portrait it moves into the sky band above the street.
- **Menus** are dark cards over the paused game, so the world stays visible behind every choice.
- **Don't:** add gradients to figures, use photographic textures, use drop shadows on canvas art, or draw outlines thicker than 2 px.

## Motion, sound and feel

Hits should feel heavy, menus should feel instant, and nothing should wait on an animation.

| Moment | Feel | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A normal hit | A tick | 3-frame hit-stop, small spark, 2 px shake |
| A crit or heavy blow | A slam | 5-frame hit-stop, big spark, 4–6 px shake, white flash at 12% |
| A boss falls | A breath | Slow motion for about a second, a ring burst, "FLATLINED" |
| Level up, feat, legendary | A chime | Gold text rising, a short arpeggio, a 3-pulse vibration |
| Menus | Instant | No slide-ins; a selected skill pops once (0.6 s) |

- **Sound** is synthesized chiptune: square and triangle waves, a minor-key bass line per district, a faster boss track. Effects are short and dry; nothing longer than half a second.
- **Haptics** on phones and controllers: short pulses for hits you take, longer for big moments. Never on the title screen.
- **Respect the player's settings:** reduced motion halves particles and stops pulsing animations; screen shake, vibration and music each have their own off switch.

## Applications

**App icon:** a runner mid-kick under a rising neon sun, on Ink. Keep the figure inside the center 80% so the maskable version survives a circle crop. No text in the icon.

**Store and install listing:**

- *Name:* Cyber City Tactics
- *Short description (80 characters):* A neon brawler RPG for one or two. Punch, shoot, cast and hack. Plays offline.
- *Long description opens with:* "Seattle, 2079. Pick a runner, take a job, and choose who you hurt." Then three short paragraphs: the campaign and co-op, the Sprawl and its choices, the secrets and the Codex.
- *Category:* Games, Action RPG.

**Screenshots:** always real gameplay at the game's own resolution, never mock-ups. One wide shot of the Sprawl, one tall shot of a fight with touch controls, one of the Codex. Turn tips off and dismiss the rotate hint first (tools/screenshots.mjs does this).

**Social posts:** lead with a moment, not a feature list: a boss at half health, a door sign turning red, a legendary dropping. Caption in the game's voice, under 20 words, then the link. Use the stacked lockup in a corner, never over the action.

**Share lines** inside the game follow one pattern: what you did, the numbers, the link. Example: "Cyber City Tactics Arena · weekly 2026-W43: 12,345 points, wave 9 (Runner, solo), best combo 22. Beat it: …"
